[Normal] Slide 1 — Title
Computational Modeling of Zein–Caseinate Nanoparticle Behavior in Dairy Protein Systems
Yi Wang, Liping Zhang, Qin Wang · University of Maryland · ACS 2026
[Normal] Slide 2 — Contents
Introduction & Objectives / DLVO Modeling / Molecular Docking / Coarse-Grained MD Outlook / Summary
[Normal] Slide 3 — Introduction: the gap
[Normal] Nanoparticle science is mature, but most work is done in oversimplified media (PBS, DI water)
[Normal] Real food matrices add ionic strength, pH shifts, competing proteins, sugars, fat — milk, yogurt, bread
[Normal] Protein corona forms immediately and changes size, charge, and stability
[Normal] Image: NP schematic + milk / yogurt / bread photos
[Normal] Slide 4 — Introduction: why computation
[Normal] Wet-lab screening is slow, material-intensive, and largely trial-and-error from literature/experience
[Normal] Objective: use computational simulation to predict how food nanoparticles behave and change in food systems
[Normal] Goal: save time and materials → rational design of nanocarriers → better nutrient encapsulation and delivery
[Normal] Three-method strategy: DLVO (bulk energetics) → docking (residue-level contacts) → CG-MD (dynamic, crowded surface)
[Normal] Slide 5 — DLVO: framework and data
[Normal] Classical DLVO = electrostatic double-layer repulsion + van der Waals attraction; V_max sets kinetic stability (>15 kT stable, 10–15 marginal, <10 unstable)
[Normal] Inputs measured by DLS/ELS: particle radius a, zeta potential, pH, ionic strength → Debye length, Grahame equation
[Normal] Measured grid: pH 3–7 × 25–100 mM, 12 conditions (zein–caseinate NPs, pH-driven method)
[Normal] Image: energy_profiles.png or vial photos
[Normal] Slide 6 — DLVO: progress and limitation
[Normal] IEP located at pH ~4.06, well below the ~5.8 reported for bare zein → caseinate genuinely reshapes surface chemistry
[Normal] CCC signature at pH 4: 246 → 441 → 8404 nm across 25/50/100 mM
[Normal] All measured and food-relevant conditions (whole milk, yogurt, bread-release) fall below 10 kT — no barrier; |ζ| ≤ 14 mV and V_EDL ∝ ζ²
[Normal] Limitation: classical DLVO ignores steric hindrance from the caseinate/casein-micelle layer, hydration forces, and non-sphericity → extended DLVO with a steric term is the next step

[Normal] Slide 7 — Molecular docking: setup
[Normal] Protein–protein docking (MEGADOCK / HDOCK), not AutoDock Vina — corona is protein-on-protein
[Normal] Whey (β-lactoglobulin, α-lactalbumin) structures available from PDB; α-zein has no crystal structure → AlphaFold/ColabFold prediction only
[Normal] Zein model quality is the bottleneck: mean pLDDT 49, cross-model Cα RMSD 13.7–21.5 Å
[Normal] Image: zein_alpha pLDDT plot
[Normal] Slide 8 — Molecular docking: a negative result with a mechanism
[Normal] Five milk proteins scored 5.16–5.82 (Z) — statistically indistinguishable; no specific binding site
[Normal] Restraining to 40 exposed residues gave only 3–6 residue interfaces (span 92.8 Å, not a contiguous surface)
[Normal] The one confident region (84–115) is a generic α-helix (0.59 Å to ideal poly-Ala) — no usable tertiary information
[Normal] Interpretation: corona formation is non-specific and multivalent — the expected biology, and the justification for CG-MD
[Normal] Image: all5_zein_partners_comparison.png
[Normal] Slide 9 — Coarse-grained MD: the outlook
[Normal] MARTINI 3 + martinize2 + GROMACS: ~4 heavy atoms → 1 bead, 20 fs timestep → hundreds of ns feasible on one GPU
[Normal] Step-by-step plan: (1) build a multi-chain zein surface patch instead of a single chain; (2) add 2–4 milk proteins above it; (3) solvate at target pH/ionic strength; (4) measure contact persistence and adsorption over time; (5) feed an effective steric/adhesion term back into extended DLVO
[Normal] What it supplies that docking cannot: a real curved crowded surface, conformational ensembles, avidity, explicit solution conditions
[Normal] Realistic near-term success: 1–2 conditions, one clean trajectory, a preliminary trend
[Normal] Slide 10 — Summary & Significance
[Normal] A three-tier computational pipeline (DLVO → docking → CG-MD) for predicting food-nanoparticle behavior in real food matrices
[Normal] Each tier answers what the one below cannot: does a barrier exist → which contacts → does the interface survive thermal motion
[Normal] Delivered so far: caseinate-shifted IEP, CCC window at pH 4, quantified instability across three food environments, and a mechanistic explanation for why rigid docking fails on nanoparticles
[Normal] Impact: less wet-lab trial-and-error, rational nanocarrier design, improved nutrient encapsulation and delivery

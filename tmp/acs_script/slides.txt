[TEXT] Why discuss nanoparticles in real foods?
[TEXT] Goals: nutrient-fortified foods — curcumin, vitamin D or ω-3 in everyday products
These bioactives are poorly soluble, unstable, and poorly absorbed on their own
Nanoencapsulation protects the bioactives and improves bioavailability
But the carrier must survive the food matrix first — ions, pH, competing proteins

===== SLIDE 4 =====
[TEXT] Why model it computationally?
[TEXT] Wet-lab screening is slow and material-intensive — one condition at a time
Objective: predict how the carrier behaves in a food system before we make it
Saves time and material → rational nanocarrier design → better encapsulation and delivery
Three levels, coarse to fine — to predict the NPs behaviors in food systems

===== SLIDE 5 =====
[TEXT] The three proteins in play
[TEXT] Whey: compact, folded, water-soluble — β-lactoglobulin (18 kDa), α-lactalbumin (14 kDa)
Casein: no fixed fold — ~80% of milk protein, assembled into ~150 nm micelles
Zein: maize prolamin, water-insoluble — 60% hydrophobic residues, only 2% charged

===== SLIDE 8 =====
[TEXT] DLVO: findings and limitation
[TEXT] IEP ≈ 4.94 (bare zein ≈ 6.2) — caseinate changes the surface chemistry
At 10 mM there is a real barrier — 17 to 49 kT across pH 4–7 (n = 3)
It collapses with ionic strength — pH 7: 49 kT (10 mM)→ 5.6 kT (10 mM)→ ≈ 0kT (150 mM)
All three food conditions stay below 10 kT; DLVO still has no steric term

===== SLIDE 10 =====
[TEXT] Docking: no protein wins, and why
[TEXT] Five milk proteins docked onto the predicted zein surface — scores are indistinguishable
Restrained interfaces were only 3–6 residues, all five converging on residues 37–39
The limit is the zein structure, not the docking — there is no reproducible fold to dock against
So the corona is non-specific and multivalent — the question moves to coarse-grained MD

===== SLIDE 11 =====
[TEXT] Coarse-grained MD: the plan
[TEXT] Build a multi-chain zein surface patch, not a single chain
Place 2–4 milk proteins above it, so multivalent contact is possible
Solvate at a defined pH and ionic strength
Run it, and measure whether the docking-predicted contacts persist
Extract an effective steric term — and feed it back into extended DLVO

===== SLIDE 12 =====
[TEXT] Summary and significance
[TEXT] Three tiers, coarse to fine — each answers what the one before it cannot
Zein–caseinate NPs are stable at low ionic strength, but no food condition clears 10 kT
Rigid docking cannot resolve selectivity on a nanoparticle — a negative result with a mechanism
Impact: less trial and error, rational nanocarrier design, better nutrient delivery
